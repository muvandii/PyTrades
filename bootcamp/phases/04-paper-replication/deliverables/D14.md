# D14 — Replication R3: Vol-Managed Portfolios + cross-paper synthesis

## Objective

Implement volatility management on a multi-asset portfolio with a causal vol estimator, test it against the replication critique, and then synthesise all three replications: what replicated, what did not, and why.

## Teaches

Replication fidelity is a spectrum, and the deviation *is* the result. Statistical significance and economic significance are different questions that need different answers.

## Build

1. **`papers/R3_volmanaged/impl.py`**
   - scaling: `target_vol / σ_t` with trailing, causal σ (20-day simple, 60-day EWMA span, 20-day squared-return — three estimators);
   - cap on the scaling factor (report the cap and how often it binds);
   - base portfolio: equal-weight, then vol-managed.
2. **`papers/R3_volmanaged/comparison.md`** — paper numbers vs yours, each gap assigned to a deviation class, plus the estimator table.
3. **`papers/synthesis.md`** — the cross-paper answer:
   - what replicated (direction, magnitude, net of costs);
   - what did not, and the **single** deviation that explains the most variance for each;
   - why, at the level of mechanism;
   - the rule you will apply to the next paper you read.

## Required experiments

- [ ] Three vol estimators, same everything else - report all three
- [ ] Turnover scaled vs unscaled (rescaled exposure trades without a signal change)
- [ ] Cap × target-vol grid (cap ∈ {1.0, 1.5, 2.0}, target ∈ {8%, 10%, 12%})
- [ ] 2× costs
- [ ] Bootstrap CI on the Sharpe **difference** (not on each Sharpe separately)
- [ ] Per-asset improvement table, so the average cannot hide the losers

## Definition of done

- [ ] `impl.py`, `comparison.md` exist; `papers/synthesis.md` answers all four questions
- [ ] The estimator choice is stated next to the number it produced
- [ ] Turnover for both versions is reported
- [ ] Statistical significance and economic significance are answered in separate sentences
- [ ] M4 checklist in `week-09.md` is fully ticked
- [ ] Graveyard entries for any vol-management rules that died (e.g. an uncapped inverse-variance rule)
