# M1 — Reality check (end of Week 2)

## Pass condition

> Debunk a viral strategy, start to written verdict, in 30 minutes (timed and logged).

Verbatim from the course registry. The timer log lives in the header of
`reports/debunks/debunk-01.md`; the verifier checks the file, the human checks the honesty.

## What it proves

That you own a repeatable procedure, not a lucky result: reconstruct the claim, reproduce its
own numbers, break it with execution / costs / sample size, and write the verdict. Thirty
minutes is the bar because it forces a checklist instead of a research project - and because
a claim you cannot check in half an hour is a claim you will never check.

## What it does NOT prove

- That every viral claim is false (some contain real, small edges).
- That you can find a profitable strategy (that is Week 6's problem).
- That your data pipeline is fast (that is Week 4's problem).

## Fail path

If you overrun 30 minutes: note the actual elapsed time, identify where the time went
(usually reconstruction - claims are vague on purpose), and repeat with a fourth claim
within 48 hours. Two consecutive overruns means your intake process is wrong, not your speed:
return to the debunk template and cut it to the smallest codable rule.

## Remediate before Week 3

Week 3 starts the engine build, which assumes Phase 1 is done: validated data, a metrics module
you trust, and the reflex to check execution before believing a number. If M1 failed, fix the
process first. Do not start the engine with a broken measurement loop; you will debug two
things at once and conclude the wrong one is wrong.
